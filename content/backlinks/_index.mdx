## What Are Backlinks?

Backlinks are hyperlinks from one website that point to another website. When Website A links to Website B, that creates a backlink for Website B. In SEO terminology, backlinks are also called inbound links, incoming links, or simply "links."

The concept is straightforward: every time another website references your content and includes a clickable link to your page, you've earned a backlink. These links serve as pathways that connect the vast network of websites across the internet.

From a technical perspective, a backlink is created using HTML anchor tags. Here's what a basic backlink looks like in code:

```html
<a href="https://yourwebsite.com/page">Anchor Text Here</a>
```

The `href` attribute contains the destination URL (your website), and the text between the opening and closing tags is the anchor text—the clickable words users see.

### Why Backlinks Exist

Backlinks were originally designed to help users navigate between related content across the web. When Tim Berners-Lee invented the World Wide Web, hyperlinks were the fundamental mechanism for connecting documents. This interconnected structure—the ability to reference and link to other resources—is what makes the web a "web."

For content creators, linking to other websites serves several purposes:
- **Citing sources** for claims and statistics
- **Providing additional resources** for readers who want to learn more
- **Crediting original research** or ideas
- **Recommending tools, products, or services**
- **Building relationships** with other creators in their space

When someone links to your content, they're essentially saying, "This resource is valuable enough that my audience should see it."

### The SEO Significance of Backlinks

Search engines recognised early on that backlinks could serve as quality signals. If many reputable websites link to a particular page, that page is likely to be valuable, trustworthy, and authoritative.

Google's founders, Larry Page and Sergey Brin, built their search engine around this concept with [PageRank](/backlinks/link-equity-pagerank-explained)—an algorithm that treated links as votes of confidence. Pages with more high-quality backlinks would rank higher in search results.

While Google's algorithms have evolved dramatically since 1998, backlinks remain one of the most important ranking factors. According to [Google's documentation on how search works](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/how-search-works), links between pages help Google discover new content and determine which pages are most authoritative. [Google's Search Quality Evaluator Guidelines](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/guidelines.raterhub.com/en//searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf) also emphasise the importance of expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness (E-E-A-T).

## Why Backlinks Matter for SEO

Understanding why backlinks matter requires looking at them from Google's perspective. Search engines face an enormous challenge: determining which of billions of web pages best answers any given search query.

### The Trust Problem

Anyone can create a website and claim expertise on any topic. How does Google determine which sources to trust? Backlinks provide part of the answer.

When established, authoritative websites link to your content, they're vouching for its quality. A backlink from a respected industry publication carries significant weight because:

1. **Editorial standards**: Reputable sites don't link to low-quality content
2. **Reputation at stake**: Sites risk their own credibility by linking to poor resources
3. **Expertise signals**: Domain experts are more likely to recognise and link to valuable content

### Ranking Impact

Multiple independent studies have analysed the relationship between backlinks and rankings:

- Websites ranking in the top positions typically have significantly more referring domains than those ranking lower
- The correlation between referring domains and rankings is among the strongest of any measurable factor
- Pages with zero external backlinks rarely rank for competitive keywords

This doesn't mean backlinks are the only factor—content quality, user experience, and topical relevance all matter. But for competitive keywords, building a strong backlink profile is typically essential.

### Beyond Rankings: Additional Benefits

Backlinks deliver value beyond search rankings:

**Referral Traffic**: Quality backlinks from high-traffic websites send visitors directly to your site. This traffic is often highly targeted, as users clicking through are interested in your specific topic.

**Brand Awareness**: When your site appears across multiple publications and resources in your industry, you build recognition and credibility.

**Relationship Building**: The process of earning backlinks often creates valuable industry relationships that extend beyond SEO.

**Indexing and Crawling**: Backlinks help search engines discover new pages and understand site structure. Pages with more backlinks are typically crawled more frequently.

## Types of Backlinks

Not all backlinks are created equal. Understanding the different types helps you evaluate opportunities and build a diversified link profile.

### By Link Attribute

The `rel` attribute in HTML specifies the relationship between the linking page and the linked page. This affects how search engines interpret the link.

**DoFollow Links** (Standard Links)

Links without any special attribute are "dofollow" by default. They pass [link equity](/backlinks/link-equity-pagerank-explained) and serve as ranking signals. These are the most valuable type of backlink for SEO purposes.

**NoFollow Links** (`rel="nofollow"`)

Added in 2005, the nofollow attribute tells search engines: "Don't associate my site with the linked page." Originally, these links passed no ranking value, but [Google now treats them as "hints"](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2019/09/evolving-nofollow-new-ways-to-identify) rather than directives.

**Sponsored Links** (`rel="sponsored"`)

Introduced in 2019, this attribute identifies paid placements, advertisements, and sponsored content. According to [Google's link spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#link-spam), using this attribute is required for compliance when money exchanges hands.

**UGC Links** (`rel="ugc"`)

Also introduced in 2019, UGC (User-Generated Content) identifies links within user comments, forum posts, and other user-submitted content.

For detailed guidance on these attributes, see our [complete guide to DoFollow, NoFollow, Sponsored, and UGC links](/backlinks/do-follow-vs-no-follow-vs-sponsored-vs-ugc).

### By Acquisition Method

**Editorial Links**

These are the gold standard—links that website owners and editors add naturally because they find your content valuable. You didn't ask for these links; they were earned through content quality alone. Editorial links carry the highest value because they reflect genuine endorsement.

**Outreach-Based Links**

Links acquired through deliberate outreach campaigns. This includes guest posting, broken link building, resource page outreach, and digital PR. When done correctly, these links are valuable and legitimate.

**Self-Created Links**

Links you create yourself in forums, blog comments, directories, or social profiles. These carry less weight and can be problematic if overdone. Focus on relevance and value rather than volume.

### By Placement

Where a link appears on a page affects its value:

**Contextual Links**: Placed within the main content body, surrounded by relevant text. These carry the most weight.

**Sidebar Links**: Appear in sidebars, often as resources or recommendations. Less valuable than contextual links but still beneficial.

**Footer Links**: Appear at the bottom of pages, often site-wide. Lower value and can appear manipulative if overused.

**Navigation Links**: Part of a site's main navigation. Useful for user experience but carry less editorial endorsement.

## How to Evaluate a Backlink

Not every backlink helps your SEO. Some provide significant value; others have minimal impact; a few might even cause problems. Learning to evaluate backlink quality is essential.

### The Four Pillars of Backlink Quality

#### 1. Relevance

The topical relationship between the linking site and your content is crucial. A backlink from a related industry publication is far more valuable than one from an unrelated site.

Ask these questions:
- Does the linking site cover topics related to yours?
- Would the link make sense to human readers?
- Does the surrounding content relate to your page?

A cooking blog linking to your restaurant is relevant. That same cooking blog linking to your software company is less so.

#### 2. Authority

The linking domain's overall strength affects the value of links from that domain. Several metrics attempt to measure this:

- **Domain Authority (DA)**: [Moz's metric](https://moz.com/learn/seo/domain-authority), scaled 0-100
- **Domain Rating (DR)**: [Ahrefs' metric](https://ahrefs.com/blog/domain-rating/), also 0-100
- **Authority Score**: [Semrush's equivalent measurement](https://www.semrush.com/kb/685-authority-score)

These are third-party estimates, not Google metrics. They're useful for comparison but shouldn't be treated as absolute measures. For more on interpreting these metrics, see our [authority metrics explained](/backlink-quality/authority-metrics-explained) guide.

Beyond metrics, consider:
- Does the site have real traffic?
- Is it a genuine business or publication?
- Does it rank for relevant keywords?
- Is the content high-quality?

#### 3. Editorial Quality

How the link was placed matters:

**Strong signals**:
- Link appears within editorial content
- Surrounding text is relevant to your page
- The linking page has genuine traffic
- Other links on the page are to quality resources

**Weak signals**:
- Link is buried in a long list of unrelated links
- The page exists only to host links
- No real traffic or engagement
- Poor writing quality or thin content

#### 4. Natural Appearance

Your overall backlink profile should look natural—like links were earned through merit rather than manipulation. This means having:

- **Diverse anchor text**: Mix of branded, naked URL, and natural phrases
- **Variety of sources**: Links from different types of sites (blogs, news, directories, etc.)
- **Geographic spread**: Links from sites in different countries (if relevant)
- **Gradual acquisition**: Links built over time, not all at once

## How Backlinks Are Built

There are two fundamental approaches to developing your backlink profile: passive earning and active building.

### Passive Link Earning

Passive link earning means creating content so valuable that people naturally discover it and link to it without your direct involvement.

This works when you:
- **Publish original research**: Data and statistics that others want to cite
- **Create comprehensive resources**: The best guide on a topic in your industry
- **Build useful tools**: Free calculators, templates, or applications
- **Share unique insights**: Perspectives or information found nowhere else

The advantage of passive earning is sustainability. Once published, great content continues attracting links indefinitely. The disadvantage is unpredictability—you can't guarantee results or timelines.

### Active Link Building

Active link building involves deliberate outreach and promotion to acquire links. This includes tactics like:

**Guest Posting**

Writing content for other websites in exchange for a link back to your site. When done well—focusing on quality publications and providing genuine value—guest posting remains effective.

**Broken Link Building**

Finding broken links on relevant websites and suggesting your content as a replacement. This provides value to the site owner while earning you a link.

**Resource Page Outreach**

Identifying pages that curate resources on your topic and requesting inclusion. Success depends on having content that genuinely deserves to be listed.

**Digital PR**

Creating newsworthy content (studies, surveys, announcements) and pitching it to journalists and publications. This can generate high-authority links from news sites.

**Relationship Building**

Developing genuine relationships within your industry leads to natural link opportunities over time. This is the slowest but often most sustainable approach.

For comprehensive guidance on these tactics, visit our [link building strategy](/link-building) hub.

### The Hybrid Approach

The most effective strategy combines both approaches:

1. Create exceptional content worth linking to
2. Actively promote that content to relevant audiences
3. Build relationships that lead to ongoing link opportunities
4. Monitor and maintain links over time

## Common Backlink Misconceptions

The SEO industry is rife with outdated advice and persistent myths. Here are common misconceptions that can lead you astray:

### Myth: More Backlinks Always Means Better Rankings

**Reality**: Quality matters far more than quantity. One hundred links from low-quality sites provide less value than ten links from authoritative, relevant domains. A natural backlink profile grows gradually with a focus on quality.

### Myth: All Backlinks Help Your SEO

**Reality**: Not all backlinks provide value. Links from irrelevant sites, link farms, or spammy sources may be ignored by Google entirely—or worse, could raise red flags about your link profile.

### Myth: NoFollow Links Are Worthless

**Reality**: While dofollow links pass more direct SEO value, nofollow links still provide:
- Referral traffic
- Brand exposure
- Relationship building
- Natural profile diversity

A healthy backlink profile includes a mix of link types.

### Myth: You Must Disavow "Toxic" Links

**Reality**: Google has become sophisticated at identifying and ignoring spam links. The disavow tool is primarily for sites that have received a manual penalty. For most sites, obsessing over "toxic" links is wasted energy.

Learn more in our [toxic backlinks myth](/backlink-quality/toxic-backlinks-myth) article.

### Myth: Domain Authority Is a Google Ranking Factor

**Reality**: DA, DR, and similar metrics are third-party estimates. Google has its own methods for evaluating sites and doesn't use these specific metrics. They're useful for comparison but shouldn't be treated as gospel.

### Myth: Reciprocal Links Are Bad

**Reality**: Natural reciprocal linking is completely normal. Businesses link to partners; bloggers link to each other's content; industry resources reference each other. Problems only arise with excessive, manipulative link exchanges.

### Myth: Old Links Lose Value Over Time

**Reality**: Link age is generally positive. Long-standing links from established sites demonstrate ongoing value and trust. There's no "expiration date" on quality backlinks.

For more myth-busting, read our complete guide to [backlink myths](/backlinks/backlink-myths).

## Key Backlink Metrics and Terms

Understanding these fundamental terms will help you navigate backlink analysis:

### Referring Domains

The number of unique domains linking to your site. This is often more important than total backlink count, as multiple links from the same domain provide diminishing returns.

[Referring Domains vs Backlinks: What's the Difference?](/backlinks/referring-domains-vs-backlinks)

### Anchor Text

The clickable text in a hyperlink. Anchor text provides context to search engines about what the linked page is about. A healthy profile has diverse anchor text distribution.

[Complete Guide to Anchor Text](/backlinks/anchor-text)

### Link Equity (Link Juice)

The ranking value that passes from one page to another through links. Higher-authority pages pass more equity; links split equity among all outbound links on a page.

[Link Equity and PageRank Explained](/backlinks/link-equity-pagerank-explained)

### Link Velocity

The rate at which a site acquires new backlinks over time. Sudden spikes or unusual patterns can raise algorithmic flags.

[Understanding Link Velocity](/backlinks/link-velocity)

## Summary

Backlinks remain one of the most important factors in SEO. They serve as endorsements from other websites, helping search engines determine which content deserves to rank.

**Key takeaways**:

- **Quality over quantity**: Focus on earning links from relevant, authoritative sites
- **Diversification matters**: A natural profile includes various link types, sources, and anchor text
- **Context is king**: Links placed within relevant, quality content carry the most weight
- **Patience pays off**: Sustainable link building takes time but delivers lasting results
- **Avoid shortcuts**: Manipulative tactics may provide short-term gains but risk long-term penalties

Building a strong backlink profile is a marathon, not a sprint. Focus on creating valuable content, building genuine relationships, and earning links through merit.
