**Link velocity** refers to the rate at which a website gains (or loses) backlinks over time. It measures how quickly your backlink profile is growing and can signal natural organic growth or potential manipulation to search engines.

## Understanding Link Velocity

### What It Measures

Link velocity tracks:
- New backlinks acquired per day/week/month
- New referring domains per day/week/month
- Rate of change compared to historical patterns
- Relative growth vs competitors

### Why It Matters

Search engines monitor link velocity because:
- Sudden spikes may indicate manipulation
- Consistent growth suggests natural acquisition
- Patterns reveal link building strategies
- Anomalies can trigger scrutiny

---

## Natural Link Velocity Patterns

### Typical Patterns

**New content launch**:
- Initial spike as content is discovered/promoted
- Gradual decline to steady state
- Periodic bumps from new references

**Established content**:
- Slow, steady acquisition
- Occasional spikes from viral sharing
- Generally stable pattern

**Viral content**:
- Sharp, rapid spike
- Quick plateau
- Followed by gradual decline

### What "Natural" Looks Like

Natural link velocity is characterised by:
- Consistency with site activity level
- Correlation with content publishing
- Mixed link quality and sources
- Gradual overall growth

---

## Suspicious Link Velocity

### Red Flags

**Sudden massive spikes**:
- Thousands of links appearing overnight
- No corresponding content or news
- Disconnected from site activity

**Unnaturally consistent rates**:
- Exactly 10 links per day, every day
- Machine-like consistency
- No natural variation

**Spike on old content**:
- Ancient pages suddenly gaining links
- No apparent reason for renewed interest
- Especially commercial pages

### What Triggers Concern

Search engines may investigate when:
- Link growth doesn't match site growth
- Patterns appear automated
- Low-quality sources dominate
- Anchor text is suspiciously optimised

## Measuring Link Velocity

### Key Metrics

**New referring domains**: Most important velocity metric

**New backlinks**: Total new links (less meaningful due to variance)

**Net change**: New links minus lost links

**Competitor comparison**: Your velocity vs market

### Tools for Tracking

- **Ahrefs**: New/Lost reports
- **Semrush**: Backlink growth charts
- **Moz**: Link tracking features
- **Monitor Backlinks**: Alert systems

### Analysis Approach

1. Track new referring domains over time
2. Note significant spikes or drops
3. Correlate with content/activity
4. Compare to competitor patterns
5. Investigate anomalies

## Healthy Link Velocity

### What to Aim For

**Consistent growth**: Steady acquisition aligned with effort

**Natural spikes**: Expected increases around content launches

**Quality distribution**: Mix of sources over time

**Proportional to activity**: More content = more links expected

### What to Avoid

**Artificial consistency**: Unnaturally smooth growth

**Explosive spikes**: Massive jumps without cause

**Disconnected from content**: Links without content to attract them

**Low-quality dominance**: Spike of spam links

---

## Link Velocity by Site Stage

### New Sites

**Expected pattern**:
- Slow initial growth
- Gradual acceleration
- Building momentum

**Concern triggers**:
- Rapid link acquisition too early
- Links before content exists
- High authority links without cause

### Established Sites

**Expected pattern**:
- Steady baseline velocity
- Spikes around content/news
- Proportional to site authority

**Concern triggers**:
- Sudden massive increases
- Dramatic slowdowns
- Pattern changes without reason

### Sites After Events

**After viral content**:
- Expected spike followed by plateau
- Gradual return to baseline
- Higher new baseline possible

**After link building campaign**:
- Should integrate smoothly
- Shouldn't create obvious patterns
- Mixed naturally with organic acquisition

## Managing Link Velocity

### For Link Builders

**Maintain natural patterns**:
- Spread outreach over time
- Don't batch all links at once
- Mix link types and sources
- Allow natural variation

**Plan strategically**:
- Time link building with content
- Build gradually for key pages
- Balance effort across site sections

### For Site Owners

**Monitor regularly**:
- Track velocity trends
- Investigate spikes
- Address drops

**Respond to issues**:
- Audit unexpected spikes
- Disavow spam if necessary
- Document unusual patterns

## Link Velocity Myths

### "Faster is better"

**Reality**: Too fast can appear manipulative. Sustainable growth beats speed.

### "Consistent daily links look natural"

**Reality**: Real velocity has natural variance. Machine-like consistency looks artificial.

### "More velocity always helps rankings"

**Reality**: Quality matters more than speed. Slow quality acquisition beats fast spam.

### "Viral spikes are dangerous"

**Reality**: Genuine viral spikes are fine—they correlate with content that earned attention. Only artificial spikes are problematic.

---

## Negative Link Velocity

### Losing Links

Links naturally disappear over time:
- Pages get deleted
- Sites go offline
- URLs change
- Content updates

### When to Worry

**Normal churn**: 1-5% loss is normal

**Concerning loss**:
- Major domain stops linking
- Large-scale link removal
- Penalty to linking sites

### Addressing Link Loss

- Monitor for significant drops
- Investigate lost high-value links
- Reclaim where possible
- Build new links to offset

---

## Summary

Link velocity is the rate of backlink acquisition:

**Natural patterns**:
- Consistent with site activity
- Spikes around content launches
- Natural variance day-to-day
- Gradual overall growth

**Warning signs**:
- Massive unexplained spikes
- Artificial consistency
- Links without content
- Spam source dominance

**Best practices**:
- Build links gradually
- Time with content publishing
- Monitor for anomalies
- Maintain natural variance

Focus on steady, quality-focused link acquisition rather than rapid growth.
