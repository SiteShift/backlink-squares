Not all link opportunities are worth pursuing. Effective link building requires systematic qualification to focus effort on prospects most likely to convert into valuable links.

This checklist provides a two-stage approach: quick filtering to eliminate poor prospects fast, and deep analysis for priority evaluation.

---

## Directory qualification: four worked decisions

These examples use official requirements checked on **9 September 2026**. They demonstrate a decision process, not measured traffic or an endorsement of every listing on a platform. Eligibility and budget are hard gates: a high domain metric cannot compensate for failing either.

| Candidate and business | Evidence | Decision | Next action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile for an online-only research shop | [Google excludes online-only businesses](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en) | Exclude | Choose a route appropriate to an online business |
| BetaList with a zero publisher budget | [BetaList says all submissions require payment](https://betalist.com/support) | Exclude on budget | Do not spend time completing the gated pricing flow |
| G2 for a consumer app still in beta | [G2 excludes B2C and alpha/beta products](https://documentation.g2.com/help/docs/finding-or-listing-a-product-on-g2) | Exclude | Revisit only if the actual product becomes eligible |
| SaaSHub for working SEO software | [The product form](https://www.saashub.com/services/submit) offered a free route in our visit | Continue to detailed review | Check category fit, description, public listing and current terms |

The fourth decision is deliberately “continue”, not “approved”. A form accepting your URL does not tell you whether a public listing will be useful. Read the [SaaSHub walkthrough](/blog/submit-product-saashub) for original screenshots of the actual flow.

### Inspect the page where a customer would find you

Open a relevant category and a recent listing, not only the homepage. Record the exact URL and a short factual observation for each question:

- Can a visitor navigate from the category to the listing without searching for its exact title?
- Does the listing explain the product or business, or merely display a keyword and outbound link?
- Are contact, correction and ownership routes understandable?
- Is the website destination accurate, and are paid placements labelled appropriately?
- Does the listing remain readable without logging in?

Use the [crawlability checker](/tools/crawlability-checker) for a bounded technical inspection and the [live backlink checker](/tools/live-backlink-checker) for a known source URL. A blocked request is inconclusive, not proof of a bad publisher. Neither tool establishes Google indexing or the number of customers using a directory.

### Save a reason that another person can understand

“Good site, DR 80” is not a qualification note. “Released B2B product qualifies; free basic profile available; no duplicate found; category page helps buyers compare invoicing tools” identifies the reasoning and what must be rechecked. That sentence is an illustrative note, not a claim about a particular accepted submission.

Use the [prospect tracker](/templates/prospect-tracker.csv) and add columns for requirements URL, checked date and published URL if your workflow needs them. Review [directories by business type](/blog/business-directories-by-business-type) before applying this checklist to a large list.

## Stage 1: Quick Scan Filter (60 Seconds)

Complete this section first. If any item fails, disqualify the prospect and move on.

### Instant Disqualifiers

- [ ] Site loads properly (no errors, not parked)
- [ ] Content is in target language
- [ ] Site appears legitimate (not obvious spam)
- [ ] Has some relevance to your content/industry
- [ ] Not a direct competitor's site

**If ANY checkbox is unchecked**: Skip this prospect immediately. Do not proceed to Stage 2.

### Quick Quality Indicators

Check these to determine if deeper analysis is warranted:

- [ ] Domain authority meets minimum threshold (DA ___+)
- [ ] Site has real content (not just aggregation)
- [ ] Updated within last 12 months
- [ ] Not obviously a PBN or link farm

**If 3+ items checked**: Proceed to Stage 2 for full evaluation.

**If 2 or fewer**: Low priority. Only pursue if prospect volume is limited.

### Section C: Content Quality Assessment

**Site-Level Content**

- [ ] Original, substantive content throughout
- [ ] Professional writing quality
- [ ] Regular content updates (activity within 90 days)
- [ ] Clear authorship information
- [ ] Proper citations and references where appropriate

**Page-Level Content** (specific page for link)

- [ ] Page has meaningful content (not thin)
- [ ] Content is well-organised and readable
- [ ] Provides genuine value to visitors
- [ ] Not primarily a link aggregation page
- [ ] Links are contextual, not just listed

**Content Red Flags**

Check for these warning signs:

- [ ] NOT thin content (less than 300 words with no substance)
- [ ] NOT obviously AI-generated without editing
- [ ] NOT scraped or duplicate content
- [ ] NOT keyword-stuffed
- [ ] NOT focused primarily on selling links

## Decision Matrix

### Go/No-Go Criteria

**Must Have (All required)**:
- [ ] Relevance score 3+ (out of 5)
- [ ] Not a spam site (E score 5+)
- [ ] Some path to getting link (F score 4+)

**Strong Indicators (2+ preferred)**:
- [ ] DA/DR 30+
- [ ] Real traffic (1,000+ monthly)
- [ ] Content quality score 6+
- [ ] Trust indicators present

### Final Decision

- [ ] **PURSUE**: Meets all must-haves + 2+ strong indicators
- [ ] **MAYBE**: Meets all must-haves + 1 strong indicator
- [ ] **SKIP**: Missing any must-have OR low overall score

## Common Qualification Mistakes

### Over-Qualification

- Rejecting good opportunities due to one imperfect metric
- Setting DA threshold too high for your domain
- Expecting perfect relevance match

### Under-Qualification

- Accepting any site willing to link
- Ignoring spam signals
- Not checking content quality

### Right Balance

- Focus on relevance and quality over raw metrics
- Consider your own domain's authority level
- Trust your judgment on borderline cases
