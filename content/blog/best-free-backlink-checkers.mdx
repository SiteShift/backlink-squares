The **best free backlink checker** depends on the question you need to answer. For your own verified site, start with Google Search Console. For competitor research, explore the public or account-based tools from established backlink-index providers. For a particular link, inspect the source page directly.

This is an editorial selection guide, not a claim that we tested dozens of products or measured a vendor's accuracy percentage. Free access, exports and account requirements change. Check each provider before relying on a specific limit.

## Choose by task

| Task | Starting point | Limitation to remember |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Review links to your own site | Google Search Console Links report | Verified properties only; report is not comprehensive |
| Inspect a competitor's profile | Ahrefs backlink checker or Moz Link Explorer | Free access can expose only a sample |
| Investigate an individual link | Source-page HTML and destination URL | Confirms presence, not ranking credit |
| Compare tool observations | Same-domain exports from available providers | Scope and collection date must match |
| Organize prospects and follow-ups | A spreadsheet | A tracker does not discover links by itself |

## Google Search Console: start with your own property

Search Console is the first place to review Google's reported external linking sites and linked pages for a property you control. Export the relevant report and keep the date. The [official documentation](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9049606) explains that this is not a comprehensive list of every link.

Use the report to identify patterns and important source domains. Do not interpret a disappearing row as proof of a penalty or a complete loss of ranking credit. Inspect the source and compare other evidence before taking action. Search Console does not give you another company's verified property data.

## Ahrefs: explore a competitor backlink sample

The [Ahrefs backlink checker](https://ahrefs.com/backlink-checker) offers a starting point for investigating a domain you do not own. Before comparing results, confirm whether you are researching a domain, subdomain or exact page. Free results may be restricted, and fuller exports may require an account or paid plan.

Use the sample to identify source pages worth reading. Check relevance, publication context and whether a link still exists. Domain Rating is a third-party comparative metric, not a Google score. See [Ahrefs vs Moz](/comparisons/ahrefs-vs-moz) for a fair way to evaluate differences.

## Moz Link Explorer: work with DA and linking domains

[Moz Link Explorer](https://moz.com/link-explorer) is useful when your workflow includes Domain Authority, Page Authority and inbound-link research. Check current account requirements and report availability. Avoid relying on older Open Site Explorer instructions as if they described the current interface.

Our [Moz walkthrough](/blog/moz-link-explorer-guide) explains how to choose scope, examine inbound links and investigate missing results. Spam Score should prompt review, not an automatic removal or disavow decision.

## Other providers: verify access before planning a workflow

Semrush, Majestic and other providers maintain their own link research products. Their public views, trials and export allowances may differ from paid accounts. If you need a particular report, check its availability directly. A trial requiring payment details is different from an unrestricted free tool.

Use our [checker launcher](/tools/free-backlink-checker) to open selected provider tools for a domain. The launcher creates destination links; it does not fetch a live backlink index or generate an independent authority score. That distinction matters when you are promising a client a deliverable.

## How to compare results fairly

Build a small reference list of known source pages, including recent and older links. Query the same target scope on the same day. Record the source URL, destination, reported status and any filtering. Then manually inspect a sample that appears in one tool but not another.

Separate three questions: did the tool discover a link, does the link still exist, and is the link relevant to your business? A large database does not automatically provide the best answer to all three. Do not describe a tool as universally most accurate based on a handful of domains.

Count referring domains separately from raw links. A sitewide navigation link may generate many rows from one publisher. See [referring domains vs backlinks](/backlinks/referring-domains-vs-backlinks) before turning an export into a report.

## A practical free review workflow

1. Export available information from Search Console for a property you control.
2. Check a relevant competitor in one available third-party tool.
3. Review the source pages of your most important links and prospects.
4. Record evidence in the [free audit sheet](/templates).
5. Investigate broken targets and publisher changes before contacting anyone.
6. Compare real organic landing-page performance with your prior reporting period.

Keep the review focused. If you are building a new prospect list, a backlink checker may reveal competitor sources, but it will not tell you whether you qualify for each publisher. The [Complete Backlink Database Bundle](/bundle) is another starting point: a CSV of 276 recorded opportunities to filter and verify, rather than a live checker.

## Common questions

### Can a free tool show every backlink?

Do not assume completeness. Vendor crawlers cover different parts of the web and free products may limit visible results. Even Google's Links report is not comprehensive. Combine sources where useful and state limitations in your report.

### Can a checker tell me which links Google counts?

No third-party authority metric establishes Google's treatment of a particular link. Use tools for discovery and analysis, then assess the page, relevance and link attributes. Avoid ranking promises based on a checker result.

### Should I remove links flagged as toxic?

A label is not enough evidence. Review the links and your actual situation before acting. Read the [backlink quality hub](/backlink-quality) and [disavow guidance](/backlink-audit/disavow-guide), rather than using a tool score as a removal rule.
