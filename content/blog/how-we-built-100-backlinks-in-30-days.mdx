## Is 100 backlinks in 30 days a useful target?

A target to build 100 backlinks in 30 days is incomplete until you define what counts, which pages need support and what your team can actually deliver. One website can produce many links without creating many independent recommendations. Counting URLs alone can reward the wrong activity.

Start with the [link building strategy hub](/link-building) if you have not yet chosen the audience and pages your campaign should support.

## Define an acceptable placement before forecasting volume

Write down the target audience, the destination page and the reason a publisher would mention it. A useful reference might answer a question, substantiate a claim or help a reader complete a task. “We need links this month” is not a reader benefit.

Record source URLs and referring domains separately. Also distinguish editorial mentions, directory listings, sponsorships and links you control. These are different acquisition methods and should not be combined into a misleading earned-media total.

Use the [backlink quality framework](/backlink-quality) to assess context. A high domain metric does not establish that a particular page has readers or that your link belongs there.

## Work backwards from capacity

Here is an illustrative planning calculation, not a conversion benchmark. Suppose a small team reserves 20 hours for research and estimates that checking one prospect takes 12 minutes. That leaves capacity for approximately 100 prospect reviews. It does not produce 100 outreach messages, because some prospects will be unsuitable, and it certainly does not imply 100 placements.

If 40 prospects pass review in this hypothetical example and tailoring a pitch takes 15 minutes, drafting alone needs another 10 hours. Replies, revisions and placement checks require additional time. Adjust the inputs using your own time records instead of borrowing a promised industry response rate.

| Stage | Record | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research | Sites inspected and time spent | Establishes the cost of finding suitable publishers |
| Qualification | Accepted and rejected prospects, with reasons | Prevents unsuitable bulk outreach |
| Contact | Relevant pitches delivered | Separates activity from a result |
| Conversation | Replies and agreed next steps | Shows where editorial work is needed |
| Publication | Verified source and destination URLs | Establishes what actually went live |
| Outcome | Referral activity and relevant search trends | Connects work with the business objective |

## Week one: prepare the asset and shortlist

Choose one useful destination rather than spreading effort across every product page. Check that it loads, is accessible to crawlers and explains its subject clearly. Identify existing internal pages that should reference it with the [internal link finder](/tools/internal-link-finder).

Next, build a small shortlist from publications your intended audience already uses. Note the exact section or article that makes the publisher relevant. Reject sites whose only apparent purpose is selling placements.

If a directory or submission opportunity fits your business, the [Complete Backlink Database Bundle](/bundle) provides a research starting point with recorded costs, submission links and notes. Check current eligibility before applying; buying the database does not buy acceptance or rankings.

## Week two: test the proposition

Send a manageable first batch of tailored pitches. Explain the useful contribution and why it fits the recipient's audience. Avoid treating a template as permission to send irrelevant messages at scale.

Review replies before expanding. If editors misunderstand the idea, improve the pitch. If the topic is already covered, find a distinct contribution or stop pursuing that publisher. A request for original examples may indicate that the asset needs more work, not that the subject line needs another variation.

Use [outreach templates](/templates) as a starting structure, then write the substantive part for each recipient.

## Week three: deliver and verify

Prioritise the conversations already in progress. Supply the agreed material, clarify attribution and record any commercial arrangement. Google's [link spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) are relevant when links are bought or arranged to manipulate rankings.

When a page goes live, check the actual destination and link attributes using the [live backlink checker](/tools/live-backlink-checker). Follow the [placement verification guide](/blog/verify-backlink-placement) before marking it complete. A screenshot or an editor's promise is not a substitute for a live source URL.

## Week four: report decisions, not just totals

Separate published placements from pitches awaiting a reply. Show how many distinct relevant publishers were reached and what time each stage consumed. Record rejected prospects too: those decisions explain why a smaller campaign can be more selective.

Compare results with the initial business objective. If the campaign produces relevant referral visits but no enquiries, inspect the landing page and offer. If research consumes most of the time, improve qualification rules. If good conversations stall on content quality, invest in the contribution before expanding the list.

The next month's target should follow this evidence. There is no requirement to preserve an arbitrary 100-link goal when the work shows a better direction.
