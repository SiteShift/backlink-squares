Free and paid directory submissions differ in more than price. You might be paying for faster review, advertising, a confirmed placement or optional profile features. Write down the deliverable before comparing offers.

We checked the examples below on **9 September 2026**. Fees and queues can change. A publisher's description of its service is not evidence that a placement will increase your rankings.

## Current examples and what the fee means

| Publisher | Observed route | What to check before choosing |
|---|---|---|
| [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/how-product-hunt-works) | Free launch | Product eligibility and personal-account requirements |
| [SaaSHub](https://www.saashub.com/services/submit) | Free or $75 one-off Priority+ in the live form | Queue, verification and promotion details; review still matters |
| [BetaList](https://betalist.com/support) | All submissions paid; no free option | Current plan price inside submission flow and selection/refund terms |
| [G2](https://sell.g2.com/profiles) | Free basic profile, paid marketing options separate | Whether your released B2B software qualifies |
| [Trustpilot](https://business.trustpilot.com/pricing) | Free account, paid plans separate | Features included in the free plan versus a paid trial |

In the SaaSHub form we observed, the free queue displayed “up to 32 days”; Priority+ displayed “up to 24h”. Those are the publisher's displayed estimates, not turnaround times measured by BacklinkGrid. The [walkthrough screenshots](/blog/submit-product-saashub) preserve the observation.

BetaList is the counterexample to outdated free-directory advice. Its support page now says payment is required and unsuccessful selections receive an automatic refund. We could not establish its current plan amounts from the public support page, so we do not quote a price. With a zero budget, the decision is already clear: exclude it.

## Separate four different purchases

**Review fee:** payment gets the submission considered under stated terms. Read the rejection and refund policy. Do not count the payment receipt as publication.

**Priority processing:** payment changes the queue or service level. Ask whether it also changes eligibility. A faster decision can still be a rejection.

**Advertising or featured exposure:** payment buys a specified promotional position or period. Check where the listing appears and for how long, then evaluate the customer audience.

**Research file:** payment buys information to help you select opportunities. Our [Complete Backlink Database Bundle](/bundle) is this category: 276 opportunities in a CSV for £11.49. Publisher fees and your submission work are separate; buying the file does not buy acceptance.

## Paying more does not establish SEO value

Google's [link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/qualify-outbound-links) asks publishers to qualify advertising and paid placements appropriately. Its [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) address buying or selling links for ranking manipulation. Do not choose a fee simply because someone promises an unqualified link.

Instead, ask whether real prospective customers use the page, whether the listing describes your business accurately, and whether you can measure a useful outcome. A paid advertising placement can be commercially sensible without being a strategy for purchasing ranking credit.

Our broader [paid versus organic link building comparison](/blog/paid-vs-organic-link-building) explains the strategic distinction. This guide focuses on the practical submission decision.

## Count the time cost as well

Use this planning equation:

**Campaign cost = publisher fees + preparation hours × your chosen hourly cost + follow-up cost.**

For example, five submissions taking 20 minutes each require 100 minutes of work. At an illustrative £30 per hour, that is £50 of time even if the publisher fees are zero. This is a hypothetical budgeting example, not a measured submission benchmark.

If the actual contribution per sale were £10, a £50 campaign would need five attributable sales to cover that cost, before any other overhead. Substitute your own margin, refunds and fulfilment costs. Revenue and profit are different measures.

## A zero-budget submission workflow

1. Exclude paid-only destinations before filling forms.
2. Search for an existing listing and claim it where appropriate.
3. Check business type and release-stage eligibility.
4. Prepare a factual description and original product screenshots once.
5. Select the explicit free route, checking for trial or renewal terms.
6. Record the confirmation and review status separately from the published link.

Save what actually happened, including restrictions and unfinished steps. This produces a useful working record without treating submission as acceptance.

## Decide using evidence, then keep it

Save the price currency, one-off or recurring basis, plan name, checked date and source URL. After publication, record the exact listing URL and check the backlink. A tool timeout should remain an inconclusive check rather than a reported rejection or removal.
