## How to choose a link building agency

The best link building agency for your business should be able to explain its methods, demonstrate relevant work and agree how you will judge the result. A package advertised by link count and domain score tells you little about audience fit or the quality of the actual placements.

**Editorial correction:** this page previously described a ranked agency review without a documented research method or evidence for its comparative claims. It now provides a due diligence process rather than an unsupported league table.

Start with your [link building strategy](/link-building) before requesting proposals. An agency brief is easier to assess when it names the audience, destination pages and business objective.

## Write a brief that makes proposals comparable

Give each provider the same information: the pages you want to support, the market, available expertise, content resources and constraints. Explain whether you need research, digital PR, content production, relationship development or placement verification.

Ask the agency to separate its fee from publisher charges, content costs and other expenses. A quoted monthly price is not comparable if one proposal includes original content while another assumes your team writes everything.

Request the following in writing:

- The acquisition methods and why they fit your audience.
- Examples of actual source pages, with client permission where needed.
- Who writes, reviews and approves contributions.
- How commercial relationships and link attributes are handled.
- The reporting fields and what constitutes a completed deliverable.
- Cancellation, replacement and ownership terms.

## Inspect source pages rather than logos

Open examples and read the article or listing where the link appears. Does the page have an intelligible purpose for a real audience? Does the destination help the reader? Is the contribution relevant or inserted awkwardly into unrelated copy?

A well-known publisher's logo does not tell you which section was used or what relationship produced the link. Likewise, a high domain metric cannot establish the quality of a contributor page on that domain. Use our [backlink quality framework](/backlink-quality) to make the review consistent.

If an agency cannot share private client work, ask for an anonymised process walkthrough or another form of verifiable evidence. Confidentiality can be legitimate; it should not require you to accept undocumented claims about results.

## Ask how links are obtained

Ask directly whether the provider pays publishers, operates sites it places links on, uses intermediaries or relies on reciprocal arrangements. The answer affects how the work should be described and evaluated.

Google's [link spam policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) is the relevant reference for practices intended to manipulate rankings. Do not accept a “white hat” label as a substitute for explaining the method. Paid advertising should be treated and qualified appropriately, not sold as guaranteed ranking credit.

Your own review should distinguish editorial work from business listings and sponsorship. All may have different commercial purposes; reporting them as identical endorsements obscures that distinction.

## Use a pilot with acceptance criteria

A manageable initial scope lets you evaluate communication, research and delivery before a longer commitment. Agree the scope with the provider rather than demanding an arbitrary number of links by a deadline.

| Area | Agree before work starts | Evidence at review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relevance | Intended audience and suitable source types | Reason each prospect fits |
| Content | Author, review process and ownership | Approved contribution or asset |
| Placement | What counts as delivered | Live source URL and final destination |
| Commercial terms | Fees and disclosure approach | Written terms and actual attributes |
| Reporting | Dates, fields and definitions | A source-level report you can verify |

Use the [placement verification checklist](/blog/verify-backlink-placement) when reviewing delivery. A blocked fetch is inconclusive, not automatically a missing link; inspect uncertain cases before disputing an invoice.

## Evaluate a case study critically

Ask what changed beyond link acquisition. Content rewrites, technical repairs, seasonal demand and broader marketing can influence results. A before-and-after traffic chart without those details does not isolate the effect of links.

Look for clear date ranges, relevant page groups and the original objective. Check whether the provider is reporting all traffic or the audience the campaign was meant to reach. A credible case study explains limitations and unsuccessful work as well as improvements.

Avoid treating another client's growth percentage as a forecast for your site. Your starting point, resources and competitive environment differ.

## Match the buying decision to the bottleneck

If your pages are technically broken or unconvincing, fix them before paying to promote them. If your team can execute outreach but spends too long collecting submission details, a research resource may solve a narrower problem at lower cost.
