The right business directories depend on how your business serves customers. A local plumber, an online shop and a B2B application should not use the same submission list. Start with eligibility, then ask whether the directory helps a potential customer evaluate you.

This guide compares four business models using official platform information checked on **9 September 2026**. It is a selection framework, not a promise that any particular application will be accepted.

## Match the listing to the business model

| Business model | First route to investigate | Prepare | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer-facing local business | Google Business Profile; eligible Yelp business page | Genuine operating details, service area or premises, ownership evidence | Fake offices, keyword-stuffed business names |
| Online-only shop | Trustpilot company profile where appropriate | Actual trading domain, ownership access, genuine customer feedback process | Claiming a local presence that does not exist |
| Released B2B software | G2 or a suitable software marketplace | Working product, accurate category and release status | Describing beta or consumer software as eligible B2B software |
| Professional services | A relevant trade association or genuine partner directory | Credentials, membership or partnership evidence | Assuming anyone can purchase eligibility |

These examples serve different jobs. A review profile helps customers inspect experiences; a trade directory may establish membership; a product marketplace supports software comparison. None should be judged solely by the number of outbound links it allows.

## Local businesses: check real-world contact first

Google's [eligibility guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en) excludes online-only businesses. For an eligible business, keep the profile aligned with how customers actually interact with you. A service-area operator and a shop customers visit need different location information; do not publish a home address just to make a profile look complete.

Before adding a profile, search for an existing one and resolve ownership rather than creating another version of the same business. Keep your trading name, phone, website and operating details consistent with reality. Use our existing [local citation building guide](/strategies/local-citation-building) for maintaining those records.

[Yelp's business page offering](https://business.yelp.com/products/business-page/) includes a free claim route, separate from advertising and upgrades. Check the platform's supported business categories and market before applying. A restaurant should prepare opening hours and useful customer information. A mobile repair service should explain where and how it operates. Neither needs a paragraph of SEO keywords instead of a clear description.

## Online shops: a customer-review profile has a different purpose

For an online-only shop, Google's local profile is not a substitute for an appropriate online business presence. [Trustpilot's pricing page](https://business.trustpilot.com/pricing) describes a free account and company-profile claim route alongside paid plans. Select the actual free plan if you have no budget; a paid-plan trial is a different commitment.

Prepare ownership access and a process for handling genuine feedback. Do not write your own customer reviews or ask only satisfied buyers for feedback in a way that violates the platform's rules. The reason to maintain the profile is to help customers assess the business, not to manufacture a positive rating.

A shop should also look for genuine supplier stockist pages. If a brand you actually sell has a retailer locator, check its inclusion requirements. This route depends on a real commercial relationship; a generic directory purchase cannot create it. Our [e-commerce link building guide](/industries/ecommerce) covers related acquisition options.

## B2B software: release status changes the shortlist

A software directory is a stronger fit for an application than for an agency or a downloadable research file. G2's [official listing rules](https://documentation.g2.com/help/docs/finding-or-listing-a-product-on-g2) specifically exclude B2C and alpha/beta products. A free basic profile does not override that restriction.

For a new product launch, investigate Product Hunt; for a software comparison profile, investigate SaaSHub. Our [SaaS submission guide](/blog/where-to-submit-saas-product) compares their different requirements and the current BetaList payment restriction. Choose the route based on your release, not on a generic “high-DR directories” article.

## Professional services: prove the relationship behind the listing

For an accountant, architect or specialist agency, start with organisations the business already belongs to. Inspect the member directory of the actual professional body, local chamber or technology partner programme. Record whether inclusion requires membership, certification, a geographic connection or an approved partnership.

For a concrete agency example, [Shopify’s Partner Directory eligibility page](https://help.shopify.com/en/partners/grow-your-business/partner-directory/join) ties inclusion to programme tier status and describes invitations for Plus, Premier and Platinum partners. An agency that builds Shopify stores should check its actual partner status; simply offering web design does not establish eligibility. For other professions, use the official body appropriate to the country and qualification.

An online-only consultancy should also avoid inventing a storefront to obtain a local listing. If customers are met in person, evaluate the applicable platform's actual rules. If they are not, choose a route that supports the real business model.
