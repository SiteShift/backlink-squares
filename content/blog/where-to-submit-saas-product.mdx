Where to submit a SaaS product depends on whether you need launch feedback, a software comparison profile or access to business buyers. Start with the product's stage and audience. A free submission to the wrong platform is still wasted work.

We checked the official routes below on **9 September 2026**. These are a deliberately small selection with different purposes, not a ranking of domain authority or a promise of backlinks. For the wider acquisition strategy, start with [SaaS link building](/industries/saas).

## Choose the route that matches your product

| Route | Best fit | Cost status checked | Main restriction |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/launch) | A usable new product or substantive launch | Free launch | Personal maker account and a product worth trying |
| [SaaSHub](https://www.saashub.com/services/submit) | Software people can compare with other tools | Free submission; optional $75 one-off priority route observed | Released English-language product on its own domain; publisher review |
| [G2](https://sell.g2.com/create-a-profile) | Released B2B software | Free basic profile | Excludes B2C and alpha/beta products |
| [BetaList](https://betalist.com/submit) | A qualifying new technology startup | Paid only; current price shown in its submission flow | Product stage, originality and landing-page requirements |

The SaaSHub price is a dated browser observation, not a permanent quote. BetaList's public support page does not provide the current plan amounts, so we have not guessed them. A launch community and a software marketplace also deliver different experiences: compare audience fit before comparing fees.

## Product Hunt: prepare for people trying the product

Product Hunt is suitable when you have something useful to demonstrate and can answer questions about it. Treat the launch page as an introduction to the product: show the task it solves and what someone can do after clicking through.

Its [official rules](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/how-product-hunt-works) distinguish usable products and substantive updates from ordinary posts or lists. Its [posting instructions](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/479557-how-to-post-a-product) require a personal account rather than a company account. A founder should own that identity; an outsourced submitter should not pretend to be the maker.

Prepare a direct product URL, concise tagline, accurate pricing and screenshots of the working experience. Our [Product Hunt preparation walkthrough](/blog/product-hunt-submission-checklist) explains the handoff. If your destination is only a spreadsheet for sale, do not assume the same eligibility as an interactive software product.

## SaaSHub: describe the software accurately

SaaSHub's product entry begins at **/services/submit**. The similarly named **/submit** page is a submission-promotion area, which can confuse someone following an old directory list. In our browser check, entering the homepage led to a form with a product name, tagline, categories, competitors, optional URLs and a contact email.

The form imported website text. Review that text: a homepage description written to sell several products may be a poor summary of the particular software you are listing. For BacklinkGrid, we described the free tools and identified the paid database and placements separately.

The form offered free review and a $75 priority option. We followed the free route while preparing the walkthrough. See the [SaaSHub walkthrough](/blog/submit-product-saashub) for original screenshots and the observed steps. The publisher's [FAQ](https://www.saashub.com/faq) also distinguishes approval, verification and paid featuring. None is interchangeable with a measured SEO result.

## G2: check release stage before completing a profile

G2's [listing documentation](https://documentation.g2.com/help/docs/finding-or-listing-a-product-on-g2) excludes B2C products and products in alpha or beta. Search for the product first; if it is already listed, follow the claim process instead of creating a duplicate. New listings are reviewed for eligibility and category fit.

The [free profile offering](https://sell.g2.com/profiles) is separate from paid marketing features. A consumer habit tracker should not be relabelled as enterprise software to get listed. A released business invoicing application has a more plausible fit, but the publisher still decides. That example illustrates eligibility; it is not an approval prediction.

## BetaList: remove it from a zero-budget shortlist

BetaList's [current support page](https://betalist.com/support) says all submissions are paid. Its [criteria](https://betalist.com/criteria) focus on new technology startups and exclude categories such as agencies and ordinary online stores. Older advice describing a free queue is no longer a safe basis for a campaign.

If your budget is zero, skip the application. If you are considering payment, inspect the current plan, the review conditions and the expected audience before committing. Read [free versus paid submissions](/blog/free-vs-paid-directory-submissions) to distinguish payment for consideration from payment for exposure.

## Build a submission pack once, then adapt it

Create one source document containing the product name, canonical URL, factual one-sentence description, pricing model, target audience, original screenshots and an authorised contact. Keep unsupported superlatives and ranking promises out of it.

Adapt the explanation to the destination. A launch audience needs to understand what is new. A comparison audience needs to understand the job the software does and where it differs from other products. Avoid adding a competitor merely to appear on its page if the products do not solve a comparable problem.

Before sending, test the destination as a new visitor. Can they try the feature you describe? Does the page explain the limits? Does the mobile layout make the next action obvious? Fix those issues before you spend effort bringing people to it.
