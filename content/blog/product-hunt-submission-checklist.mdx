A Product Hunt submission needs a clear product story, an authorised personal account and a destination people can use. Prepare those before choosing a launch date. A directory-style description focused on getting a backlink will not help someone understand why they should try the product.

This is a preparation walkthrough based on Product Hunt's official guidance checked on **9 September 2026**. We did not complete an authenticated Product Hunt launch or test acceptance. The instructions are based on the linked publisher documentation; the browser presented a security check, so we do not show a submission-screen screenshot.

## Check the product and account first

Read [how Product Hunt works](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/how-product-hunt-works). A meaningful product launch or substantial update is different from posting an ordinary list. Check previous listings before preparing a new launch; use the publisher's current relaunch rules if the product already exists there.

The [official posting instructions](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/479557-how-to-post-a-product) require a personal account. Prepare the launch under the real maker's identity and complete the required onboarding. Do not create a company persona masquerading as a person.


## Prepare an asset pack that explains one useful job

Write a one-sentence answer to “What can a visitor do with this?” Then choose screenshots that demonstrate that answer. For a link-checking tool, show the input, the result and the explanation of what the result means. A dashboard with no visible task tells a weaker story.

Use this working checklist:

- A direct, functioning product destination.
- The product's actual name and a concise tagline.
- A factual description and accurate pricing model.
- Original screenshots of the available experience.
- The maker's identity and a useful explanation of why the product exists.
- A list of known limitations that might matter to someone trying it.

Check the current image and text requirements in the official form when you enter them. Prepare editable originals so you can adjust crops and wording without starting again. Avoid screenshots of planned features presented as available functionality.

## Make the description specific

A weak description says “The ultimate platform to transform your growth.” It gives a reader nothing to evaluate. A stronger description identifies the action, audience and output: “Check a known page for your backlink and inspect its anchor and link attributes.” That is an illustrative description of a narrow tool, not a claim of a complete backlink database.

If a site offers free tools and paid products, distinguish them. Someone arriving for a free checker should not need to buy a database to perform the advertised task. Someone considering the paid file should be able to see what they receive.

For BacklinkGrid, the [tools directory](/tools) and [bundle page](/bundle) serve those different purposes. Choose the destination that matches the particular product you are launching. Do not imply that buying a CSV gives access to a live external backlink index.

## Test the visitor journey before launch

Open the destination without being signed in. Use a mobile viewport and a realistic example. Confirm that the primary action works, errors are understandable, and the result has enough context to be useful.

For a crawler-based tool, explain the crawl limit and what cannot be concluded from a failed request. For a download, show the file format and a real sample. For a paid purchase, make the price and deliverable clear before checkout. These checks improve the experience regardless of whether the launch generates a large audience.

Keep the first screen focused. A visitor should be able to understand the tool, use it and discover the relevant paid next step without navigating through multiple promotional panels.

## Enter the launch and review the preview

Follow the current [Product Hunt launch guide](https://www.producthunt.com/launch) after signing in. Review the preview for cropped images, unclear wording and mismatches between the launch description and landing page. Save a draft if the experience is not ready.

Prepare an opening explanation that adds context rather than repeating the tagline. Describe the problem you encountered, the scope of the solution and the kind of feedback that would help. Do not invent customer numbers, tests, testimonials or performance improvements to make the launch sound established.

Make time to answer substantive questions once published. Product Hunt's community rules prohibit manipulation such as paying for votes; distributing a useful product should not depend on manufacturing engagement.

## Measure behaviour after the click

Separate visitors, tool completions, bundle visits, purchases and refunds. A launch-page vote is not revenue, and a referral visit is not a purchase. Use the attribution your analytics actually supports; do not claim every sale during a launch came from it.

Record the public launch URL in the [prospect tracker template](/templates/prospect-tracker.csv), then verify any backlink independently. If you are choosing between launch destinations, compare [SaaS submission routes](/blog/where-to-submit-saas-product) by audience and eligibility.
