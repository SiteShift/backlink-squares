## How to Use This Guest Posting List

### Evaluating Opportunities (The DRAFT Framework)

Before pitching any site, evaluate using DRAFT:

**D - Domain Authority**
- DA 70+: Premium, competitive, high link value
- DA 50-69: Sweet spot for most link builders
- DA 30-49: Easier placement, still valuable if relevant
- DA under 30: Usually not worth the effort unless hyperrelevant

**R - Relevance**
A DA 40 site in your exact niche typically outperforms a DA 70 site in an unrelated field. Google values contextual relevance heavily.

**A - Audience**
Does this site's audience match your target customers? Guest posting isn't just about links—it's about reaching potential customers.

**F - Follow Status**
Verify dofollow links in author bio. Use browser inspection or a tool like MozBar to check existing guest posts on the site.

**T - Traffic**
Check SimilarWeb or Ahrefs for estimated traffic. Real traffic = real referral potential beyond SEO.

### Before You Pitch

1. **Read 5+ recent guest posts** on the site
2. **Study the guidelines** word-for-word
3. **Check their content gaps** - what haven't they covered?
4. **Prepare 3 unique topic ideas** that fit their style
5. **Have writing samples ready** in similar formats
6. **Follow editors on social** and engage genuinely

## How to Write a Winning Guest Post Pitch

### The 5-Part Pitch Framework

After analyzing 500+ successful pitches, here's what works:

**1. Personal Connection (1-2 sentences)**
Show you know their content. Reference a specific recent article and share a genuine reaction.

**2. Your Unique Angle (2-3 sentences)**
Explain what you'll cover that they haven't. Be specific about your perspective or data.

**3. Credibility Signal (1-2 sentences)**
Why should they trust you? Mention relevant experience, publications, or credentials.

**4. Topic Options (3 bullet points)**
Offer 3 headline options with one-line descriptions. Let them choose.

**5. Easy Out (1 sentence)**
Make it easy to say no: "If these don't fit, I'd love to know what topics you're looking for."

### Sample Pitch Template

```
Subject: Guest post idea: [Specific Topic] + [Unique Angle]

Hi [Editor Name],

I loved your recent piece on [specific article]. [One genuine observation about it].

I'd love to contribute a piece on [topic] that covers [unique angle] - something I haven't seen covered on [Publication Name]. I've [credibility: run campaigns/managed teams/achieved results] in this area.

Three potential angles:

1. [Headline 1]: [One-line description]
2. [Headline 2]: [One-line description]
3. [Headline 3]: [One-line description]

Here are a few recent pieces I've written: [link 1], [link 2].

If these don't fit your current editorial calendar, I'd love to know what topics you're looking for.

Best,
[Name]
```

## Common Guest Posting Mistakes

### 1. Generic Pitches
Using template emails without personalization. Editors receive hundreds of these weekly.

### 2. Ignoring Guidelines
Not following submission instructions exactly. Immediate rejection signal.

### 3. Self-Promotional Content
Writing thinly-veiled advertisements. Editors detect this immediately.

### 4. Poor Topic Selection
Pitching topics already covered or irrelevant to the audience.

### 5. Weak Author Bio
Missing the opportunity to include a compelling CTA and proper links.

### 6. No Follow-Up
Giving up after one pitch. Polite follow-up after 2 weeks is appropriate.

### 7. Quantity Over Quality
Chasing DA numbers instead of relevance and audience fit.
