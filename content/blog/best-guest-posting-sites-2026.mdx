## Choose guest posting sites by editorial fit

The best guest posting sites for your business are publications whose readers can use your expertise. A large list of domain scores does not establish whether a publication accepts your subject, permits your proposed link or has capacity for a contribution.

**Editorial correction:** the earlier list on this URL claimed 67 verified opportunities, personal pitching experience, response-rate benchmarks and monthly metric checks without supporting evidence. Those claims have been removed. Below are two researched examples and a repeatable process for finding suitable publications in your own field.

For the full campaign process, see our [guest posting guide](/link-building-tactics/guest-posting). This page focuses on selecting and checking a publisher before writing the pitch.

## Two official contributor routes to study

These official guidelines were reviewed on 9 September 2026. They describe editorial submission routes, not guaranteed acceptance or guaranteed backlinks. Read the current page before contacting an editor.

| Publication | Potential fit | What to prepare | Official instructions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Smashing Magazine | Practitioners with useful web design or development experience | A focused outline, intended audience and evidence of expertise | [Writing a Smashing article](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/write-for-us/) |
| A List Apart | People with an argument about web design, development or content practice | A pitch and outline or draft supported by evidence | [Contributor guidelines](https://alistapart.com/about/contribute/) |

Smashing Magazine's guidance asks for original work and rejects obvious product promotion. It describes an editorial process involving an individual author and revisions. A walkthrough whose main purpose is selling your software is a poor fit, even if the software relates to web development.

A List Apart asks for a clear argument and supporting evidence for people who make websites. Its guidelines discourage sales pitches. A thoughtful explanation of a design decision with concrete lessons is more aligned with that brief than a generic list of SEO tips.

These examples are deliberately narrow. Neither is a general submission destination for every industry. Use the same checks for a specialist trade publication, professional association or niche editorial site closer to your audience.

## Find prospects beyond “write for us” lists

Start with sources your customers already read. Look at bylines and contributor pages, then search the publication for the subject you want to address. A site may accept expert contributions without using the phrase “guest post.” Conversely, a page using that phrase may simply be selling links.

Useful searches combine a precise subject with an editorial route: a trade term plus “contributor guidelines,” “pitch an article” or “editorial submissions.” The [link prospect finder](/tools/link-prospect-finder) builds searches you can inspect yourself; it does not verify a publisher for you.

Save the guideline URL as well as the homepage. Record the date checked and the section that explains eligibility. This avoids wasting time on opportunities copied from an old roundup after their submission process has changed.

## Qualify the actual publication

Read recent articles in the section where your contribution would appear. Check whether there is a recognisable audience, identifiable authors and an editorial purpose. Inspect linked references in context. A domain-level score is supporting information, not permission to ignore the page itself.

Use a simple decision record:

| Question | Evidence to save | Reject or pause when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does our expertise fit? | Relevant section and example article | The topic connection is forced |
| Is there a submission route? | Official guidelines and contact method | Only an unverified intermediary offers access |
| Can we contribute something distinct? | Proposed lesson, evidence or example | The pitch repeats an existing article |
| What commercial terms apply? | Written terms and link policy | Someone sells ranking credit or concealed sponsorship |
| Can we deliver the work? | Named author and draft schedule | The team cannot support revisions or evidence |

A rejection at this stage saves writing time. Do not convert an unsuitable editorial opportunity into a paid link negotiation simply to preserve a prospect count.

## Write a contribution an editor can assess

Lead with the proposed lesson and intended reader. Explain what evidence or experience you will bring, then provide a short outline. Reference a relevant section of the publication without flattering the editor or pretending to have read work you have not read.

For example, a developer might propose a practical comparison of two migration approaches using an actual project they can document. They should explain the decisions and limitations, obtain permission to share project details and supply evidence. They should not invent a success story to create a stronger hook.

Use our [guest post pitch templates](/resources/guest-post-pitch-templates) for structure. Follow the publisher's requested format when it differs from the template.

## Agree attribution without demanding ranking credit

Ask how author attribution and relevant references are handled. The publisher decides what belongs in the article. Do not assume every accepted contribution includes a followed commercial link.

Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) apply to links arranged primarily to manipulate rankings. Where sponsorship is involved, appropriate qualification matters. Read our [link attribute guide](/backlinks/do-follow-vs-no-follow-vs-sponsored-vs-ugc) before agreeing a paid arrangement.

When published, check the actual page with the [live backlink checker](/tools/live-backlink-checker) and record the destination and attributes. Keep editorial acceptance, publication and verified placement as separate stages.

## Build a shortlist you can maintain

Keep the list small enough to revisit. Update closed routes, note unsuitable subjects and remove entries that no longer have a clear editorial fit. Measure the time spent and the quality of conversations rather than importing an unsourced acceptance benchmark.
