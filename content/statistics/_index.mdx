**Link building statistics** are useful when they help you make a better decision. A percentage without a clear denominator, source and observation period can be misleading. This library explains how to measure a campaign and interpret its results without treating industry averages as promises.

## Start with the question

If you want to understand your site's profile, begin with [backlink statistics](/statistics/backlink-statistics). If you want to improve a campaign, separate [outreach response rates](/statistics/outreach-response-rate-statistics) from [guest-post publication outcomes](/statistics/guest-posting-statistics). If you want to evaluate spending, use [pricing measurements](/statistics/link-building-pricing-statistics) and [contribution-based ROI](/statistics/content-marketing-roi-statistics).

These are different units of analysis. Mixing them into one score hides useful information. A campaign can receive many replies but few relevant placements. Another can produce a useful listing without a measurable ranking change. Report the stage that was actually observed.

## What these guides contain

Each guide defines a metric, provides a formula, works through an illustrative calculation and explains its limitations. Example numbers are not BacklinkGrid customer results, a new survey or universal targets. We have removed unsupported claims that appeared to cite studies without a verifiable source.

When using an external study, follow the link to the original publication. Check its collection period, sample selection, geography and definitions. A roundup dated this year can still be quoting research collected many years earlier. Preserve that distinction when presenting the data.

## A useful reporting structure

1. **Inputs:** research time, content work, tools and publisher costs.
2. **Activity:** qualified prospects, messages, submissions and follow-ups.
3. **Intermediate outcomes:** relevant replies, accepted ideas and verified publications.
4. **Business outcomes:** qualified visits, leads and attributable contribution.
5. **Uncertainty:** incomplete reporting, open work, changing tool coverage and attribution limits.

Keep source URLs and evidence alongside your counts. Use the [free tracking templates](/templates) to record what happened, when and what remains to do. The [backlink audit hub](/backlink-audit) explains how to investigate the links behind the report.

## Avoid false precision

A site score is not a Google ranking score. Read [Domain Authority statistics](/statistics/domain-authority-statistics) before comparing vendors or businesses. Likewise, [anchor text percentages](/statistics/anchor-text-statistics) describe a sample; they do not establish a safe optimization ratio.

Small samples can change dramatically after a single outcome. Show the underlying counts as well as percentages, and compare completed cohorts using the same method. If a campaign has not matured, label it as ongoing rather than forcing an early winner.
